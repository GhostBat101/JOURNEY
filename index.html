TAKE THE LONG WAY.

An independent editorial publication dedicated to the open road, forgotten roadside Americana, analog photography, and the quiet romance of driving with nowhere in particular to go.

The coast is
still out there.

Highway 1 clings to cliffs of decomposed granite 800 feet above the surf. Turn the radio dial past static into quiet. The Pacific is not a destination; it is an unblinking witness to every mile you chose not to measure.

Stories worth taking the long way for

The Loneliest Neon

Two gas pumps and a flickering vacuum-tube motel sign between Austin and Ely. You drink stale drip coffee under fluorescent tubes while gravel settles outside.

Red Rock & Cast Iron

A 1968 coupe running warm through sandstone canyons. The engine echo bounces off thousand-foot canyon walls like thunder rolling down a hallway.

Where the Pines Touch Salt

Wipers on low. The road smells of wet cedar needles and diesel. Cape Perpetua rises through the gloom like the spine of some ancient sea mammal.

The Pit Stop Philosophy

Stainless counter stools, a hand-lettered cherry pie board, and a waitress who remembers the logging trucks before the highway got paved in ’74.

Somewhere between here and there

Red Mesa Trading Post

Cold spring water, cedar firewood bundles, and hand-woven blankets hanging from barbed wire fences.

Timberline Junction

Where the asphalt gives way to packed gravel. Altitude 6,800 ft. Night temperatures drop forty degrees.

Go somewhere unfamiliar

Three selected corridors where road conditions invite you to put your phone in the glove box and let the miles pile up without explanation.

Pacific Coast

From Morro Bay through Carmel. 90 miles of cliffside curves, ocean spray, and zero cellular service.

The Southwest

Red dirt washes, juniper scrub, and two-lane blacktop that shimmers in the heat like standing water.

The Backroads

Old county roads where the center line disappeared forty years ago. Cattle guards and abandoned silos.

Things seen from the passenger seat

Analog contact sheet. Scratches, dust, light leaks, and roadside stops captured on Medium Format & 35mm film across five states.

From the journal

The road after midnight

When the high beams carve out eighty feet of highway at a time and every radio station fades into Mexican folk music or midnight preachers, driving ceases to be transit and becomes contemplation.

The cars we grew up with

Station wagons with rear-facing third rows, bench seats of split vinyl, crank windows that never quite rolled all the way up, and the heavy thud of steel doors that sounded like bank vaults closing.

Pull over here

The gravel pullout that wasn’t on the map. The historical marker with bullet holes. The apple tree growing alone beside an irrigation canal with no house in sight for three miles.

Where will you
go next?

The tank is half full. The sun is three fingers above the horizon. Pick an exit you have never taken.
